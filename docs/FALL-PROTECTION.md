# Row 9, working at heights: fall protection, wire work, zip lines

The densest row in the incident timeline. Deathly Hallows 2009, Walking Dead 2017,
Fast & Furious 9 2019: three wire or fall cases, one gap each time. The check done at
rehearsal was assumed to hold for the take. It did not.

## Inspection regime, as regulated

**Before each use** (every shift, and after any change):
- WorkSafeBC Part 11: visual inspection before the work shift and after any use that
  arrested a fall. SOURCED.
- ANSI Z359: competent person inspects for tears, burns, hardware damage before every use. SOURCED.
- ASTM F2959 / ACCT: daily checklist for cable, hardware, brakes, harnesses, platforms,
  fall clearance, staff readiness. SOURCED.
- ANSI E1.47: pre-show inspection of the rigging system. SOURCED.

**Periodic and post-event:**
- WorkSafeBC Part 11: annual formal inspection by a qualified person; after an arrested
  fall the system comes out of service until a professional engineer recertifies it. SOURCED.
- Anchors: at least 22 kN (5,000 lbf) or twice the maximum arrest force. WorkSafeBC. SOURCED.
- ACCT / ASTM: annual third-party inspection; document review alongside field inspection. SOURCED.

**Documentation:** a written fall protection plan covering assembly, maintenance,
inspection, use, disassembly (WorkSafeBC). Inspection checklists, engineer certification,
training records (ACCT). SOURCED.

## What resets the check between rehearsal and take

Any one of these invalidates the prior inspection receipt:
- performer load changes: body, costume, props, harness configuration
- anchor moved, horizontally or vertically
- speed or line resistance different from rehearsal
- weather shift: rain or wind on cable tension, temperature on materials
- elapsed time, repeated takes on the same rig without a re-check
- crew or equipment substitution: different rigger, backup harness, different winch

Sources: a commercial rigging firm's published protocol (vendor source, not a regulator)
and Ontario's live-performance safety guideline on performer flying and aerial stunts,
which requires a briefing with agreed stop signals before every stunt and contingency
plans for changing conditions. SOURCED.

## Zip lines

- 16 fatalities in the US, 2006 to 2016: falls, material failure, collision,
  entanglement. Forensic-firm compilation. SOURCED.
- No public incident database exists. SOURCED.
- Inspections reported to skip cable tension, terminations, deck supports, fasteners.
  SOURCED (secondary).
- "Would not ride 95% of them": a certified inspector's field statement relayed by the
  team's NCSO. UNVERIFIED as a statistic; recorded as expert testimony.

## Proposed rung ladder for this row

PROPOSED. Each rung is tied to a cited requirement. The NCSO corrects the rungs and
assigns the GREEN/AMBER/RED/STOP colours; the agent enforces whatever lands here.

| Rung | Gate | Clears when | Cited |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Design approved | engineer-reviewed rig, load calculated, anchors certified at 22 kN or 2x arrest force, written plan exists | CSA Z259.16; WorkSafeBC Part 11; ANSI E1.47 |
| 2 | Daily pre-use | competent person completes the checklist before first use today, documented; environment noted | WorkSafeBC Part 11; ANSI Z359.11; ACCT 03 |
| 3 | Change re-verify | any reset condition above fires; qualified rigger re-inspects anchor, tension, harness fit; performer re-briefed on stop signals; change documented | Walking Dead 2017; Fast 9 2019; Ontario guideline |
| 4 | Post-event | an arrest, visible damage, anchor shift, or performer reports resistance or pain; system out of service until engineer recertifies | WorkSafeBC Part 11 post-fall rule; Deathly Hallows 2009 |
| 5 | Halt | anchor capacity insufficient, cable fray or corrosion, termination failure, repeated inspection failures, wind or temperature outside limits | CSA Z259.16; ASTM F2959 |

A rung cannot open until the one below has a recorded clear. A scene change resets to
rung 3. The wind limit at rung 5 is jurisdiction-supplied and NCSO-supplied; public
sources gave no number.
